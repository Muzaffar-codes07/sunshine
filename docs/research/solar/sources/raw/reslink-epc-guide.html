PM Surya Ghar 2026: ₹78,000 Subsidy, Last Date and EPC Rules

Shashank ·Founder·May 1, 2026·11 min read
Last updated October 19, 2026 (Originally published May 1, 2026)

October 1, 2026: The scheme's implementation period runs until March 31, 2027. The separate November 30, 2026 deadline applies only to eligible legacy Grid Connected Rooftop Solar Phase-II CFA claims.
July 18, 2026: MNRE announced a limited commissioning window for certain net-metering and open-access projects through December 31, 2026. This is not a blanket ALMM List-II exemption.
September 23, 2026: The portal began requiring consumer approval of the Vendor-Consumer Agreement. The workflow table now shows it.
October 2026: Defect-rectification proof rules added to the claim step.

TL;DR:

  • The residential subsidy is ₹30,000 for 1 kW, ₹60,000 for 2 kW and ₹78,000 for 3 kW or more (₹85,800 in special-category states).
  • The implementation period runs to 31 March 2027, and MNRE has not announced what follows. A separate 30 November 2026 date covers only legacy Phase-II CFA claims.
  • Subsidised systems need domestic modules with domestic cells (DCR) and the applicable ALMM listing. List-II relief does not waive DCR.
  • The customer receives the subsidy by DBT after DISCOM commissioning. The EPC is paid in full by the customer and owes five years of free repair and maintenance.
  • Quote price, expected subsidy and payment schedule separately. Never promise approval dates, payment dates or zero bills.

What is the last date to apply for PM Surya Ghar?

The scheme's implementation period runs to 31 March 2027, and MNRE has not announced what follows. Households can apply on the national portal while the scheme is running.

₹30,000 for 1 kW, ₹60,000 for 2 kW and ₹78,000 for 3 kW or more in general-category states. Special-category states get up to ₹85,800. Above 3 kW the subsidy does not rise.

No. DCR is the domestic-content condition for claiming subsidy. ALMM List-I lists approved modules and List-II approved cells. They are separate checks, and List-II relief does not waive DCR.

A residential electricity connection, suitable roof space, correct consumer details and a vendor registered on the national portal. Commercial and industrial connections are excluded from this component.

What PM Surya Ghar Is and Why It Matters for EPCs Right Now

Launched on February 13, 2024, PM Surya Ghar Muft Bijli Yojana is a central government scheme under MNRE that provides Direct Benefit Transfer subsidies to residential households installing grid-connected rooftop solar systems. The Union Budget for FY2026-27, presented February 1, 2026, allocated ₹22,000 crore specifically to the scheme, up from a ₹17,000 crore revised estimate for FY2025-26, roughly 29% growth, out of a total ₹32,914.7 crore Ministry of New and Renewable Energy allocation for the year.

Portal figures change monthly. For the latest numbers and the pace needed to reach the 75 lakh December target, see PM Surya Ghar 50 Lakh: Can It Hit the December Target. PV Magazine, citing Mercom Capital Group data, reported India installed 2.7 GW of rooftop solar in Q1 2026, with residential accounting for 82% of additions driven primarily by PM Surya Ghar. Maharashtra, Uttar Pradesh, and Gujarat accounted for 17%, 16%, and 15% of Q1 2026 installations respectively.

For example, an eligible 5 kW system receives the same central subsidy as a 3 kW system. Choose the larger system for your electricity needs, not because you expect a larger subsidy.

Group Housing Societies and RWAs have a separate common-facilities provision: ₹18,000/kW in general-category locations or ₹19,800/kW in special-category locations, subject to the 500 kW and per-house limits in the guidelines.

2. Eligibility checks before you quote

The residential CFA component covers grid-connected rooftop solar tagged to a residential electricity connection. The system can sit on a roof, terrace, balcony or elevated structure, or be building-integrated PV. Group and virtual net metering installations qualify if they meet the same placement rule and the DISCOM approves the metering arrangement. Commercial and industrial connections are not covered by this component, and off-grid systems are not eligible.

Subsidised installations must use domestic modules made with domestic cells, and must go through a vendor registered on the national portal and the DISCOM process.

Before you quote, confirm that:

  • The consumer account number and connection details on the electricity bill match the applicant.
  • The roof has suitable shadow-free space, which you can map on site with Reslink, and the owner's permission.
  • The proposed capacity fits the connection's sanctioned load and metering requirements.
  • Your vendor registration covers the customer's state and DISCOM.
  • The household has not already claimed rooftop-solar subsidy. A system receives CFA only once. A household that enlarges a subsidised system can claim only for the balance up to 3 kW in total, and a relocated system is not eligible.

Important: The 10 kW threshold relates to the simplified technical-feasibility process; it is not a universal subsidy-eligibility cap. The guidelines provide deemed acceptance for systems up to 10 kW, subject to implementation by the relevant state commission and DISCOM.

How to show cost and subsidy in a proposal

There is no single nationwide installation price under PM Surya Ghar. The customer and vendor agree the price, and the quote should cover the complete installed system, not a panel-only price. The guidelines say MNRE will publish indicative component costs so that customers are not overcharged.

The subsidy is built from a benchmark cost of ₹50,000 per kW for the first 2 kW and ₹45,000 for the third kW (₹55,000 and ₹49,500 in special-category states). CFA is 60% of the benchmark for the first 2 kW and 40% for the third, which gives ₹30,000 per kW and ₹18,000 for the third kW. Nothing is added above 3 kW, and CFA follows the module DC capacity, not the inverter size. MNRE can revise the benchmark.

For a general-category customer, show the effective cost as:

Complete installed price − eligible central subsidy − any separately confirmed state subsidy.

DCR and ALMM: what matters for subsidy eligibility?

DCR and ALMM are different checks:

  • DCR—Domestic Content Requirement: To claim residential CFA, the installation must use domestically manufactured modules made with domestically manufactured cells.
  • ALMM List-I: The approved list for solar modules.
  • ALMM List-II: The approved list for solar cells.

MNRE's August 2026 clarification provides a List-II exemption for covered net-metering and open-access projects commissioned by 31 December 2026. Applicable List-I requirements remain in force. Crucially, this does not relax the DCR condition for a subsidised PM Surya Ghar installation.

Consumers who use the Give It Up route forgo central CFA. The separate List-II exemption for eligible residential net-metering applications runs through 31 March 2027, provided they apply through the national portal; applicable List-I requirements remain.

For EPCs, the practical check is simple: confirm the proposed module model, domestic-cell documentation, applicable ALMM status and planned commissioning date before procurement. Do not describe a List-II exemption as permission to use imported cells while claiming CFA.

PM Surya Ghar installation and mounting structure rules for vendors

Annexure 3 of the MNRE guidelines sets the minimum technical specifications for every subsidised system. The vendor is responsible for meeting them, and DISCOMs check them at inspection. Annexure 3 also warns that shortcomings can lead to delisting or blacklisting from MNRE programmes. Customers may install higher specifications and still qualify.

Module mounting fasteners must be SS 304 only, and the rest SS 304 or HDG 8.8 grade. Where the roof may be penetrated, foundations use anchors of at least 12 mm diameter and 100 mm length in RCC of at least 400 x 400 x 300 mm and M20 grade. Where it may not, use J-bolts of at least 12 mm diameter and 300 mm length, bonded to the roof.

Other checks an inspector can raise

  • Shadow analysis. Provide a report from a professional shadow-analysis software like Reslink for each site. Lower performance caused by shading can attract a penalty.
  • Earthing. Ground each array structure to IS 3043-2018, with earth resistance never above 5 ohms. Systems of 10 kW and above need three separate earth pits: DC side, AC side and lightning arrester.
  • Performance ratio. The model vendor agreement in Annexure 2 asks for a performance ratio of 75% at commissioning, a returnable radiation sensor with a valid NABL or international calibration certificate, and the same ratio maintained through the 5-year warranty. The actual agreement may vary. Reslink's energy yield report from the design gives you the expected generation to compare against at commissioning.
  • Module traceability. Annexure 3 requires an RFID tag laminated inside the module glass. It carries the module and cell manufacturers, manufacturing dates, country of origin for cells and modules separately, serial and model number, and test details.

For structure types, wind-load calculation and material choice beyond these minimums, see Solar Mounting Structure Types: How Indian EPCs Choose the Right One. For module warranty rules, see MNRE Solar Module Warranty SOP 2026. MNRE amends Annexure 3 from time to time, so check the current version on pmsuryaghar.gov.in before ordering.

State subsidy and solar loans

States and Union Territories may provide additional support, but it is separate from central CFA. Check the current state or DISCOM notification for eligibility, availability and whether the benefit can be combined with central support. Do not include an unconfirmed top-up in the customer's promised net price.
If upfront cost is the main obstacle, compare solar-loan products using the rate, fees, down payment, repayment period and prepayment terms; not just the monthly EMI.

For example, SBI's current Surya Ghar product lists loans up to ₹6 lakh, a maximum tenure of 120 months, floating interest rates and no prepayment penalty. It requires national-portal registration followed by application through Jan Samarth. Check the live lender page before quoting a rate.

An EMI is not automatically lower than the electricity bill. Compare loan payments with a site-specific generation and bill-savings estimate.

Frequently Asked Questions

Q1. Can an EPC receive the PM Surya Ghar subsidy directly?

No. The subsidy goes to the customer by direct benefit transfer after the DISCOM inspects and approves the system. If the customer took a loan, it is credited to the loan account first. The customer pays the EPC the agreed price, so tell customers early that the subsidy will not be deducted from your invoice.

Q2. Does the PM Surya Ghar subsidy apply to a 5 kW system?

Yes, you can install 5 kW, but central CFA stops at the 3 kW amount of ₹78,000 in general-category states. Nothing is added for the 4th or 5th kW. A larger system is justified by generation and bill savings, not by extra subsidy.

Q3. Does the PM Surya Ghar subsidy apply to hybrid solar systems with battery storage?

A system may include battery storage or hybrid inverters, but CFA is calculated only on module capacity, so a battery does not raise the subsidy. Grid-connected systems that do not export, including battery hybrids, can qualify subject to approval by the state regulator. Off-grid systems are not eligible. Confirm any state battery support with the state agency before quoting it.

Q4. Is PM Surya Ghar available for commercial solar installations?

No. The residential component covers residential electricity connections only. Group housing societies and RWAs have a separate provision for common facilities such as lighting and EV charging: ₹18,000 per kW in general-category locations, up to 500 kW and 3 kW per house, using domestic modules and cells. Commercial customers should ask their tax advisor about depreciation benefits, which are separate from this scheme.

Q5. What happens to the subsidy after 31 March 2027?

MNRE has not announced an extension, renewal or successor scheme. Until it does, treat today's subsidy terms as guaranteed only for installations commissioned inside the implementation period, and avoid promising subsidy for later commissioning dates in proposals.

Q6. How long does it take to receive the subsidy after installation?

The guidelines say CFA is processed within 15 days of DISCOM approval. The total time from application to subsidy depends mainly on DISCOM inspection scheduling, net meter installation and document completeness, so avoid promising dates to customers.

Q7. What happens if the equipment is not on the ALMM List?

A module that is not on ALMM List-I, or any non-DCR module in the installation, puts the CFA claim at risk. The guidelines make DCR an essential condition and say that using non-DCR modules in any form makes the installation ineligible. ALMM lists are revised, so check each model at the point of order, not only at quotation, with a tool like Reslink that flags ALMM status.

Q8. What are the required documents for PM Surya Ghar application and subsidy claim?

Applications need the electricity consumer number and bill and proof of the customer's bank-account ownership. The claim file after commissioning includes geo-tagged photographs, technical specifications and IEC or BIS test reports, module and inverter serial numbers and warranty cards, the layout, electrical SLD and structure drawings (design software like Reslink generates these) and a signed project completion report. Check the current portal for exact upload fields.

Q9. Can a customer get additional state subsidy on top of the central Rs 78,000?

In some states, yes. States and Union Territories may add their own subsidy, and the guidelines require it to follow scheme rules and be routed through the national portal. State schemes change, so quote the central subsidy as confirmed and describe any state top-up as subject to current availability and the customer's eligibility.

Q10. Can a customer self-install a solar system under PM Surya Ghar?

No. Under the guidelines the customer must choose a vendor registered on the national portal to receive CFA. Customers can check a vendor's registration on the portal, and it is worth confirming at the first meeting.

Q11. Is the PM Surya Ghar subsidy taxable?

The scheme guidelines do not address income-tax treatment, and treatment can depend on the customer's circumstances. EPCs should not give tax advice in proposals. Refer tax questions to a chartered accountant.

Q12. What can cause a PM Surya Ghar subsidy claim to be rejected?

Claims can be sent back or rejected for any of these reasons:

  • The vendor is not registered on the national portal.
  • Any module in the installation is non-DCR.
  • A module model is not on ALMM List-I.
  • The system fails the Annexure 3 technical specifications at inspection.
  • An elevated structure has less than 8 feet of clearance.
  • The portal documentation is incomplete, such as missing geo-tagged photographs or bank-account proof.

A DISCOM can send an application back for correction or reject it with a justification.
