## Review criteria for the assembled folder

1. Product profiles name the buyer, category, relevant capability, source, observed limit and uncertainty. Avoid unverified pricing where it is not needed.
2. Capability matrix separates 'vendor describes', 'documented configurable path', 'unknown' and 'confirmed unavailable'. We have little or no evidence for the last label.
3. Pain evidence distinguishes installer service failures, government delays, reported software defects and hypothetical adoption barriers. Date, geography and sample matter. Indian service studies do not establish specific ERP failure.
4. Gap records state whether the gap is a documented process problem, an unknown competitor feature, or an untested opportunity hypothesis.
5. Proposed solutions state who benefits, the action they take, the expected effect, what software cannot fix, and how the benefit would be checked.
6. Baseline PDF obligations must not be presented as new market differentiation. Keep essential survey/kit/serial/warranty/job reporting separate from opportunity bets.
7. Do not imply the folder contains exhaustive market data, verified buyer demand or complete competitor testing. A useful researched shortlist is achievable; 'all products' is not.
8. Do not introduce new architecture work into this folder. Earlier architecture drafts are background and remain unselected.

## Solution opportunities worth testing

**A. Clear service responsibility and scope.** Published service research supports unclear ownership and inconsistent scope. Let a support worker state coverage, included/excluded service and next responsible party with the supporting record. Expected benefit: fewer bounced cases. Validation: use several actual anonymized service cases and check whether different staff reach the same defensible routing decision. Competitor policy-depth gap is still unknown.

**B. Exception ownership across handoffs.** A next-action queue can reveal a survey, delivery, commissioning or subsidy step waiting on someone. Expected benefit: fewer internally forgotten steps and clearer customer updates. It cannot make a DISCOM process faster. Validate time-to-identify-owner and record completeness against the current workflow, not an invented percentage improvement.

**C. Durable customer handover.** An exported installation record can keep serials, documents and agreed scope accessible without the vendor's portal. Expected benefit: less dependence on staff searching old records. Validate that a customer/support worker can retrieve the right proof from the export. Whether competitors already provide equally useful exports remains unknown.

**D. Service cost visibility on the original job.** Track actual after-sales cost separately from customer charge and supplier reimbursement. Expected benefit: a clearer picture of lifecycle margin. Validate reconciliation on a completed installation plus a covered repair. Existing project-cost systems may already support this; differentiate only if field testing shows a usability/workflow gap.

These are hypotheses for our design, not claims of exclusive features. They can be useful even if incumbents already implement them.
